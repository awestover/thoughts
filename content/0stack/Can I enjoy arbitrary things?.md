**Conjecture**: I can hack myself to feel excited about doing lots of things. 

Fairly recently I would have strongly endorsed the above conjecture (e.g., wrt careers).

The rational is, feeling good about doing something is plausibly mostly a function of being good at that thing and acting in a way that's consistent with being excited about it. With a little bit of practice / experience, you can get good at lots of things. It probably also helps if you feel that what you're doing is important. 

I've now lost some confidence in this claim. At the very least it's harder than I'd expected to be excited about something new, and [[akrasia|hard to want to be]].

The math is a bit confusing here. For instance, if I thought that trying to work on important things multiplied your expected positive impact on the world by 1000x compared to not trying, all else equal, but that I'd be 50% less "productive" working on something that I didn't enjoy as much, then that'd still be 500x expected value. But maybe the value from trying to do good is heavy tailed, so the numbers are off. And maybe the numbers are just made up.

I don't think I have anything particularly good to say about this topic. I guess my 3 cents are:  

0. The choice between doing good things and doing fun things feels like it should be a false dichotomy. 
1. I'm extremely grateful that there are some people that have chosen to do good, even if it was hard and required re-thinking their conception of self. For instance, [Paul Christiano](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/s5sy3qiknFs7ehsLA/the-value-of-theoretical-research) at one point in time was an MIT student who enjoyed math + TCS and was planning to do TCS research because it's what he was good at. He's gone on to do a lot of really different stuff, and I suspect he's personally responsible for boosting Pr(future is good) by >1%. I think a similar thing can be said about most people that are trying to make  the AI situation go well -- they aren't "saints" that only desire to do good[^1]. They're often people that had some prior intuitions about how they'd spend their life, but have radically updated based on reasoning, even when [[on feelings|emotions]] might discourage them to break from the status quo. They're willing to take high personal risk actions, because they are clear about what their [[goodness(universe)|objective function]] is, and have decided to optimize it.
