I think it's good for certain people in your life to play a unique function for you that could not be replicated by anyone else you know. "You're the only friend with whom I can engage in this particular shade of banter." For less specific functions such as "make me feel cared about" it's better if there are multiple people that can fulfill this role.

However, the someone playing a unique and highly valuable to you role in your life is distinct from you "needing" that person. I'm happy to say "my life would be much worse if you were gone", but not "my life would not go on if you were gone".

Sometimes we do actually need other people. For instance, imagine that you are having a seizure. I'd say you "need" your friend who is sitting on the couch to call 911 for you. Need in the sense of "this is requisite for my continued existence". This is not a desirable situation to be in. It might be a meaningful experience to call 911 for your friend when they have had a seizure. Perhaps in utopia we will sometimes let people call 911 for their friends, assuming that their friends will go to heaven if they die (or maybe you should just use a pzombie, as they almost certainly do in a sim).

Need is still a valuable concept. For instance, it can be appropriate to say "in order for me to generally trust you, you need to keep your commitments to me".

Needing things from others is an unenforceable rule. It puts you at large risk (perhaps even guarantees) that you will be hurt, and doesn't have benefit compared to other approaches.

One defense that people put forth in favor of needing other people is that it's helpful to distinguish between things that would be nice if someone did them for you, vs things where you would consider it a deep betrayal for someone not to do the thing for you. Being clear about what you'd consider a deep betrayal is nice and all, but distinct from needing other people.

Furthermore, this is kind of a separate point, but I think it's really nice and valuable when people do things for you even though they didn't "need" to.
