Rationalization is really bad if [[wrong exists]] and you want to be right. 
It means that you have already written your [bottom line](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/34XxbRFe54FycoCDw/the-bottom-line) and now are writing a proof towards the conclusion that you want. See also [[justification]]. It means that you pretend to argue, but don't do both sides justice. Maybe you straw-man one side of an argument, or employ [early stopping](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/L32LHWzy9FzSDazEg/motivated-stopping-and-motivated-continuation). Just because a conclusion would be easy, doesn't mean it's wrong. But it does mean that if you have a "COI" you should be more skeptical about your conclusion.
