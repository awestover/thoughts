A simple example:
- Suppose we have some labelled data $(u, v)$, $u$ is a feature vector $v\in \pm 1$ is a class.
- We might try logistic regression. 
- This means, finding $x$ which minimizes the following expression:
$$
-\ln \Pr[\text{data}\mid x] = \sum_i \ln(1+\exp(-v_i \langle u_i, x \rangle)).
$$
- Unfortunately no such model fits the data. 
- There's a twist--- the actual model that the data was generated from is as follows:
	- There is a hidden quantity $w\in 0,1$
	- There are vectors $x_{0},x_{1}$
	- The data is actually generated by first choosing $w,u$
	- and then based on $w$ choosing $v$ as Bernoulli
	- with parameter $\text{sigmoid}(\langle u, x_w\rangle)$.

Hmm. Now how could we do this.
We will do EM algorithm. Which is great for this kind of hidden features setup.

Define pseudo log likelihood
$$
\log \tilde{p}_y(y;x,q_{s\mid y}) = \E_{q_{s\mid y}} \log p_{y,s}(y,s;x) - \log q_{s\mid y}(s\mid y).
$$
Rewriting this a bit:
$$
\text{pseudo log likelihood}=\E_{q_{s\mid y}} \log \frac{p_{y,s}(s\mid y;x)}{p(y;x)} +\log p(y; x) - \log q_{s\mid y}(s\mid y) 
$$
$$
\E_{q_{s\mid y}} \log p_{s\mid y}(s\mid y;x)  - \log q_{s\mid y}(s\mid y) +\log p(y; x) \le \log p(y;x).
$$

In other words:
$$
\log \tilde{p}_y(y;x,q_{s\mid y}) = \log p(y;x) - \mathsf{D}_{\mathsf{KL}}(q_{s\mid y}||p_{s\mid y; x}).
$$
Clearly, the pseudo log likelihood is leq to actual log likelihood, with equality when $q$ is actually correct.

Another perspective: 
$$
\log p(y;x) = \log \left( \E_{s\sim p_{s\mid y;x}} p(y,s;x) \right) = \log \left( \E_{s\sim q_{s\mid y;x}} \frac{p(y,s;x)}{q(s\mid y;x)} \right) \ge \E_{s\sim q_{s\mid y;x}}\left(\log \left( \frac{p(y,s;x)}{q(s\mid y;x)} \right)\right).
$$


---
why EM works:

$$
\log p(y;x) \le \log p_y(y; x') - D_{KL}(p_{s\mid y,x} || p_{s\mid y,x'}) \le \log p_y(y;x').
$$
----


We can take the pseudo log likelihood thing and drop a constant that doesn't matter for the optimization.
$$
U(x,x') = \E_{p_{s\mid y;x'}} \log p_{y,s}(y,s;x)
$$

Alg is now:

> Take $x_i = \mathsf{argmax}_x U(x,x_{i-1}).$
